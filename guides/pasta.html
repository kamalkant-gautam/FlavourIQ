The Pasta Guide

From a handful of flour and water to hundreds of shapes — what pasta is, how it's made, where it came from, how each shape looks and feels, and which sauce belongs with it.

The Foundation

What Is Pasta?

Pasta is an unleavened dough of wheat flour and water (or eggs), shaped, then cooked in boiling water. Simple ingredients, endless variety — the shape, the flour and the sauce decide everything.

Durum Semolina

Coarse flour from hard durum wheat. High in protein and gluten, it gives dried pasta its golden colour and firm "al dente" bite.

Soft Wheat (00)

Finely milled, silky and tender. Mixed with eggs, it makes delicate fresh pasta such as tagliatelle and filled pastas.

Water or Eggs

Dried pasta is usually semolina and water only. Fresh egg pasta uses roughly one egg per 100 g of flour for a rich, supple dough.

Other Grains

Whole wheat, buckwheat, chickpea, lentil and rice flours make wholegrain and gluten-free versions. Colour can come from spinach, beetroot, tomato or squid ink.

Fresh Pasta (Pasta Fresca)

Egg and soft flour, rolled and cut by hand or machine. Soft and tender, cooks in 2–4 minutes. Best with butter, cream and light sauces. Short shelf life.

Dried Pasta (Pasta Secca)

Semolina and water, extruded and slow-dried. Firm, holds its shape, cooks in 8–12 minutes. Keeps for up to two years and carries robust, oily or tomato-based sauces.

Through the Ages

A Brief History

Ancient roots

Noodle-like doughs appear in many early cultures. Etruscan and Roman tables knew lagana, a flat baked sheet that is a distant ancestor of lasagne.

~12th century — Sicily

Dried pasta is documented in Sicily, where durum wheat grows well. The geographer al-Idrisi describes strands of dough made and exported near Palermo.

13th–14th century

Dried pasta travels by sea from ports like Genoa and Naples. Its long shelf life suits ships and trade. The Marco Polo story is a popular myth — pasta was already in Italy.

17th–18th century — Naples

Mechanical presses and drying racks make pasta cheap and plentiful. Naples becomes the pasta capital.

19th century

Tomato sauce joins pasta, creating the pairing now seen as classic. Factories scale up production.

20th century onward

Pasta spreads worldwide with migration, with regional shapes plus wholegrain and gluten-free versions.

From Wheat to Plate

How Pasta Is Made

Milling

Durum wheat is cleaned and milled into coarse semolina.

Mixing

Semolina and water (or eggs) become a crumbly dough, kneaded until smooth and elastic.

Shaping

Dough is rolled into sheets and cut, or pushed through a die. Bronze dies leave a rough surface that holds sauce; Teflon dies give a smoother finish.

Drying

Dried pasta is dried slowly under controlled heat and humidity, from hours to a couple of days, for strength and flavour.

Resting & Packing

Fresh pasta is rested, cut and used or chilled quickly. Dried pasta is cooled and packed.

Cooking

Boiled in well-salted water to al dente, then finished in the sauce with a splash of starchy pasta water.

The Golden Rule of Pairing

  • Thin strands (spaghetti, capellini) — light oil, garlic and thin tomato sauces.
  • Flat ribbons (fettuccine, tagliatelle, pappardelle) — cream and slow-cooked meat ragù.
  • Tubes and ridges (rigatoni, penne) — chunky sauces and bakes that fill the hollows.
  • Twists and shells (fusilli, conchiglie) — pesto, chunky vegetables, cold salads.
  • Tiny shapes (orzo) — soups and light dishes.

Good Habits

Cooking Pasta Well

Salt the Water

About 10 g per litre. It should taste lightly of the sea — the only chance to season the pasta itself.

Plenty of Water

A large pot at a rolling boil so pasta moves freely. No oil needed.

Al Dente

Taste a minute before the packet time. Tender, with slight firmness at the centre.

Save Pasta Water

The starchy water binds sauce to pasta. Add a ladle while tossing for a glossy finish.

Finish in the Pan

Move pasta into the sauce for the last minute so flavours marry. Do not rinse.

Serve Right Away

Pasta keeps cooking and firms up fast. Plate hot, cheese and herbs at the last moment.
